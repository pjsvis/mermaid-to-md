## What was learned

- **The Protocol's lexicon is a working toolset, and it ate its own dog
  food.** The tagline was resolved not by taste but by applying two lexicon
  entries — the moat question and the Derrida question — to the project's
  *own* positioning. Decision 001 cites both by name. A lexicon that stays
  decorative is entropy; one that gets cited in a real decision is
  anti-entropy. This is the proof the Conceptual Lexicon earns its place.

- **A noun that keeps needing qualification is the wrong noun.** "Whiteboard"
  required a qualifier at every attempt (the who, then the where). The
  qualifications were the symptom of a substrate misnamed as the product.
  The fix wasn't another qualifier; it was naming the practice
  (`state diagrams for agent–human discussion`) and demoting the substrate
  to a supporting clause. Diagnostic: if your tagline's second clause
  exists to rescue the first, kill the first.

- **Build the non-moat part knowingly.** Decision 001 concluded "the secret
  is the practice, not the rendering." The next move was to build the
  rendering (Phase 1). Not a contradiction — the rendering is necessary
  substrate for the practice — but a discipline point: building the
  replicable part first is fine *if you know it's not the moat*. The danger
  is building it and then mistaking it for the moat.

- **Verify the sibling before inheriting its convention.** Brief 001
  instructed "same binary path resolution as `mermaid-extract.sh`," assuming
  the sibling worked. It didn't — its path was stale post-spin-off. The
  assumption was inherited, never verified, and surfaced mid-implementation
  as a bug. Generalises: *"do X like the existing thing"* is only safe if
  the existing thing runs. A phase handoff that carries unverified
  assumptions produces exactly this class of bug. This is the entry point
  for the phase-handoff discussion that follows this debrief.
