## What was learned

- **Categorise by nature, not origin.** The moat question got trimmed because
  it *appeared* in eval contexts; it belongs in the lexicon because of *what it
  is* (a meta-question). The cargo-install error was the same shape: `glow` got
  `cargo install` because it *sat next to* `just` (surface similarity), not
  because of *what it is* (Go, not Rust). Map/territory errors come from
  classifying by where a thing surfaced rather than what it is. The corrective
  is the same in both cases: re-derive from the authoritative source (the
  tool's nature / the term's function), don't copy the sibling's classification.

- **Meta-questions are tools, not domain vocabulary.** The moat question and
  the Derrida question share a structure — both operate one level above the
  object-level decision, both are cheap to ask and expensive to answer, and
  that asymmetry is the point. They belong in the working lexicon regardless of
  where they first got exercised. Trimming them by origin is a category error;
  the bar for keeping them is "is this a reusable diagnostic," not "was this
  cited in this repo's docs."

- **The agent-human loop inverts elision/deferral conservatism.** Because
  correction is per-turn (high latency) not per-breath (low latency), agents
  should elide sparingly (no breath to rescue an over-elision) and defer
  rigorously (the turn boundary is a clean decision point, but a deferred
  decision must be recorded or it dies). The Protocol's locus tags, wrap-up,
  and anti-ceremony are this inversion engineered into the operating system.
  See `playbooks/elision-and-deferral.md`.

- **A meta-lesson is real iff it can be captured as a playbook whose worked
  example is drawn from the conversation that produced it.** The descent from
  "ontological orthogonality mechanism" to "two tools, elide sparingly, defer
  rigorously" is the falsifiability test: if it can't come down to plain
  practice, it was atmosphere, not insight. The worked-example constraint (the
  "proce of fish" typo was actual behaviour, not a constructed vignette) is what
  stops the descent from being faked.

- **Doc rot is a missing feedback path, not a discipline failure.** The wrapper
  shipped in one session; the docs lived in another session's context; the turn
  boundary dropped the "now update the docs" thread. The fix isn't "remember
  better," it's "make forgetting expensive" via a check. `just check-docs` is to
  doc-rot what `td handoff`'s `uncertain` slot is to inherited assumptions — a
  structured place for the forgotten thing to surface. Same pattern as the whole
  session: engineer the feedback path, lean on git for what's recoverable.

- **`version@repo` is the minimal sufficient versioning convention.** It
  doesn't solve multi-copy drift (reconciliation is deferred); it makes each
  repo's Protocol instance uniquely identifiable, which is the least property
  that matters. The date is redundant with git blame but kept for humans. The
  real value: it stops a repo's `SYSTEM.md` from false-claiming canonical
  status. Uniqueness, not parity, is the bar.
